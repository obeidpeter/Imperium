# Twelve-chapter gameplay review

This is a source-based review of the complete campaign during the overhaul, not a human enjoyment assessment. The twelve chapters, twenty-five encounters, authored decisions, recurring cast, staged scenes and historical boundaries remain. The pass changes the rules and information around those encounters rather than replacing their narrative outcomes.

| Chapter and earned step | Distinct playable problem | Persistent stakes already present | What this pass changes or preserves deliberately |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — The Oath; Tiro → Miles | Four-action drill, then clear the boundary road and its announced reinforcement. The oath and lead seal follow training. | Truth about the javelin versus solidarity with Lucan changes standing, resources and later recollection. | Preserve the gradual ability gate and original oath. Add concise mission/action guidance and visible field plans; revised enemy decisions make terrain and protection relevant in the first field assignment. |
| 2 — First Blood; Miles → Immunis | Assist and extract through the marsh, then hold the rally ground. Carry requires reaching Lucan; Leave substitutes a survivor and a controlled extraction. | Lucan’s survival is an explicit choice; Sabinus replaces him after Leave. The company record persists. | Preserve the meaningful rescue branch. Revised pursuit, morale and positioning operate on the existing causeway and escort layouts; objective guidance explains why chasing every enemy is unnecessary. |
| 3 — Under the Eagle; Immunis → Optio | Scout concealed ground, recover records, then escort their bearers through a second line. | Claiming or sharing credit changes Crispus’s relationship to the service record later argued in Rome. | Preserve recovery versus extraction as separate objectives. Public intents omit hidden enemies; terrain and ranged lines matter when choosing an approach. |
| 4 — Hold the Line; Optio → Centurio | One command per turn: hold a gate, then move surviving squads through a wall sector. | Withdrawal preserves soldiers; holding the outwork protects stores and official honours at a human cost. | Make the first command more legible through allied intentions and explicit Order choices. The revised Withdraw order offers a direct way to regroup; no order grants an extra action. |
| 5 — The Centurion; Centurio → Primus pilus | Three commands and formations solve split tower approaches, followed by a defensive evacuation window. | Prioritising civilians creates Severianus’s transport debt; countermine priority has a different public cost. | Formation identity now depends more on terrain and neighbours: line screens ranged damage, shield wall protects its front, and wedge trades exposure for attack on suitable ground. Existing two-part mission and debt remain. |
| 6 — The Broken Standard; Primus pilus → Praefectus legionis | Rally supports a retreat and the eagle’s extraction. Save reaches Varro’s isolated guard; Obey follows the shorter eagle route. | Varro’s fate is signalled before selection, with later absence branches and the remembered lesson preserved. | Preserve the authored fate and bounded veteran recovery. Revised morale and allied regrouping give Rally tactical use. Both missions remain extraction problems; their perceived repetition still needs player observation. |
| 7 — A Legion to Lead; Praefectus legionis → Praepositus | First officers’ Contest, then preparation, deployment and capture of legion objectives. | Warning, refusing or joining the conspiracy changes the record and possible immunity debt; Gallienus still dies. | Introduce bloc preferences and cooperative Persuade before Promise exists. Public rival plans and the coalition display explain the deadline. Preserve five-command authority and meaningful preparation options. |
| 8 — Beyond the Frontier; Praepositus → Acting commander | Negotiate terms, win the ford’s Contest, extract one or two columns, then hold the far bank. | Families versus soldiers changes escort burden, Aelia’s testimony and the land pledge; negotiation also changes resources. | Material Promise now has a smaller immediate gain and a clearly priced three-supply debt; brokers challenge promised supporters. Preserve the longest chapter’s council/escort/hold variety. Its duration needs human pacing evidence. |
| 9 — Rome’s Sword; Acting commander → Dux | Choose a stratagem against the armoured wing, then secure the city approach. | Mercy versus punishment changes public confidence, army reward and the later meaning of surrender. | Preserve all three operational preparations and six-command authority. Revised cavalry approach, morale, flanks and formation terrain make the existing cavalry field more than a damage comparison. |
| 10 — The Province; Praeses → completed governorship | Allocate six convoys and enter a consistent judgment; protect stores and supply routes, then defend the policy in the tribunal. | River and farm allocation alter morale and store integrity; town allocation and tenure judgment alter specific starting blocs. Earlier debts become due. | Keep the existing concrete province consequences. Exact Address and Threaten previews show all affected delegates; bloc affinities make the tribunal’s coalition more distinct. Rebut protects speeches or Resolve without changing anyone’s vote. |
| 11 — The Senate; candidate → Senator | Two different seat layouts and issues: service audit and admission petition, requiring Record and Denounce. | An independent case versus Severianus’s patronage affects backing and a nine-supply appointment debt. Earlier service and rival testimony return. | Prosecutors now evaluate vulnerable speakers and consequential withdrawal instead of alternating by turn parity. Record favours Veterans and service interests; coalition promises remain an alternative. The decisive vote supports imperial admission, not automatic self-election by the Senate. |
| 12 — The Purple; Senator → Augustus | Win legitimacy, then complete the chosen military, defection, settlement, surrender or indebted-backing confrontation. | Standing opens routes; open obligations, surviving companions and early choices shape accession and the epilogue. | Apply the revised broker/coalition problem before the confrontation; preserve deadline voting and the minimum-support recovery route. Keep governor → senator → emperor gates, original oath reprise and exactly-once commits. New legitimacy promises can be settled in the camp ledger; otherwise they remain open debts in the accession record. |

The earlier story-stage work is deliberately retained: arrival, decision aftermath, current encounter briefing, oath and promotion are selected from actual state, reading position survives reload, and a later briefing does not replay the chapter introduction. Veteran service and bounded recovery remain a small persistent record, not a permadeath management layer. No random allied action overrides a named companion’s authored fate.

The most material change across the career is a clearer transition from personal survival, to orders and formations, to coalition building and civil responsibility. This is an implementation claim: the abilities, objectives and consequences are present in source. It is not yet evidence that a first-time player feels that progression or meets the 90–150 minute target. Chapters 6, 8 and 11 are useful human-playtest checkpoints for repetition, length and political learning respectively.
