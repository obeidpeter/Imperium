# Real-time Windows playtest improvements

22 October 2026. Based on the Windows interface assessment of PR #15, implemented on `codex/realtime-playtest-polish` from merged main `f1894f6`. Real time remains an opt-in development mode; the existing turn-based campaign and save formats remain supported.

## Changes

| Playtest finding | Implemented response |
| --- | --- |
| Keyboard targets kept their original distance, health and ambush forecast | A separate selected-target readout reads the current world five times per second, including the selected ability. Fallen, hidden and unavailable targets are handled explicitly, without silently selecting another enemy. Continuous updates do not repeatedly announce themselves to screen readers. |
| Pause stopped working after using the command list | Gameplay shortcuts are handled across the field and its controls. Native button activation, text entry, selectors, dialogs and browser shortcuts retain their keys. Movement remains tied to scene focus; releasing a held movement key also works after a modifier change. |
| Retry retained loss and targeting messages | Retry clears encounter-specific target, pointer, held-key, lesson, warning, clock, command-list and announcement state before presenting the restored encounter. |
| Controls and lessons covered the characters | Mission information, the compact unit/ability dock, pause indicator, lessons and conversations have reserved layout space outside the canvas. Narrow layouts scroll compact control rows instead of covering the fight. |
| Rescue instructions did not match the available actions | The objective strip follows Bind wounds, Carry and escort progress, names missing survivors and commander extraction, highlights endangered protected allies, and explains the actual cause of defeat. Rescue encounters initially pause for planning. The rescue guide advances through treatment and extraction. |
| Equipment and training choices lacked a visible consequence | Kit and Character panels show a rotatable model of the worn outfit. Item comparisons and next-attribute-point previews use the real combat and chamber calculators. No hypothetical comparison changes the save or consumes randomness. |
| Dark characters, crowded overlays and unclear attacks | More readable fill light, less aggressive occlusion/vignette, selected-target brackets and fewer per-man squad rings. Wind-up/contact/recovery cues follow simulation ticks; blocks, hits and stagger have distinct reactions. Four bounded deterministic separation sweeps resolve crowded bodies, redistributing blocked pushes at walls and aligning carried survivors afterward. |
| Board edges broke the horizon | Detailed ground extends beyond the expanded real-time map and joins the horizon with matching height/material treatment. Roads continue beyond exits. Live horizon framing follows the nearby exchange instead of fitting the entire expanded map. |
| Optional work and orders were easy to miss | The mission strip includes the optional opportunity countdown and queued/delayed/delivered order status. Contextual squad teaching explains screening and flanking; existing shield, positional and rescue rules supply the consequences. |
| Political targeting and outcomes were hard to read | Exact speech restriction/range explanations, stronger reachable-space circles, a persistent vote count with a conditional next-rival forecast, stable delegate medallions, bloc colours and visible agreement/dissent poses. Keyboard target details refresh after movement and speeches. Follow/horizon cameras fit both participants in a conversation while respecting an explicitly chosen overview, manual drag and reduced motion. |
| High quality was expensive | High shadow resolution is capped at 2048 rather than 4096 and ambient-occlusion samples reduced from twelve to ten. Actual Windows frame-rate results must be distinguished from software-rendered correctness benchmarks. |

The preview and field share the same equipment-aware character rig. Generated bodies remain where the baked outfit fits. Custom combinations use the existing articulated kit/anatomy rig so helmets, shields, weapons, armour and chosen appearance can be represented. The result remains stylized; it is not a new set of photoreal character assets.

## Verification

Final production build: `453872cbaca5`. Automated browser checks use legal campaign checkpoints and visible settings/import/start controls; no chapter unlock or victory is injected. Scenario-specific unit tests are separate from legal campaign runs.

- Focused control, mission, guided-opening, Curia, character-panel and graphics suite: **31 passed**.
- Full Node suite: **1,055 passed**, zero failed, skipped or cancelled. A separate post-separation real-time suite passed **184 tests**, including deterministic replays and save continuations. The interrupted earlier sequential full run is not counted as a pass.
- All four release legal autoplay scripts and the real-time campaign reached their intended completion, including Chapter XII. Asset verification, character-asset audit, performance-mesh freshness and character-production audit passed. The production audit covered 54 samples, 13 clips and 72 keyframes.
- Windows interface spot-check: equipped the Lorica hamata through the Kit panel, verified the displayed comparison and changed model, then entered Chapter V in real time and inspected follow/horizon views with the revised dock. In the final Chapter VII build, selected Gaius Faustus, followed the exchange, walked four metres without spending a speech, and used Persuade to change his stance from +2 to +4; the panel then explained the spent speech precisely. These are legal checkpoint samples, not a complete manual campaign playthrough.
- Crowd trace: all ten legal Chapter I–V encounters still won. Chapter V wall pair-ticks with centre separation below 0.5 m fell from **512 to 0**; pair-ticks penetrating the 0.7 m collider separation by more than 0.05 m fell from **9,246 to 329**. Four bounded sweeps cannot guarantee perfect separation in impossible or tightly constrained spaces.
- Simulation CPU samples on this Windows host: maximum p95 across three 1,200-tick samples was **0.085 ms** for 15 actors, **1.645 ms** for 82 actors and **1.018 ms** for six squads, below the existing 4 ms budget. These are Node simulation samples, not GPU frame rates.
- Twelve-chapter scene inventory: **13 scenes passed**, five additional behavior/accessibility checks passed, no browser/runner errors, and every exported save remained unchanged. Both Chapter V scenes ran at Balanced; the other eleven at Performance. This inventory used production revision `143f63b1ba21` before the final HUD, live civic framing and crowd fixes; it exercises the unchanged static chapter renderer through the legacy command-campaign path. It is not a real-time campaign or hardware performance certification. Chapter V used 120–124 draws and 474,597–479,537 triangles; the other scenes used 82–149 draws and 148,220–422,440 triangles under SwiftShader.
- Chromium smoke: all **16 scenarios verified** on the final build. The full run passed 15; the hearing test reused a tribunal screen coordinate captured before opening the conversation panel. After scrolling its document into view and recomputing the tribunal position, the targeted hearing rerun passed with a real drag, speech consumption and recorded case. No product change was needed and no browser errors were reported.
- Playtest-specific Chromium smoke: **16 checks passed** on `453872cbaca5`, with no page or console errors. Coverage includes live range/detection forecasts, Pause from a focused command-list button, Home clearing the previous target, the rescue's initial pause, Chapter V command/Controls/horizon layouts, and council conversations at 580 × 700 and 1,280 × 900. The equipped-model check verifies loading, control interactions, stable geometry, closing, unchanged battle ticks and preservation of an existing pause.
- Final stage shader gate: **4 of 4 configurations passed** on `453872cbaca5` (Chapter V mine and Chapter XII confrontation, each at Performance and Balanced). No browser errors, no programs compiled in the first visible frame or later in play, and generated bodies were present. Program counts were 51 / 54 / 52 / 56 against the existing 60-program budget. Draw calls were 115 / 119 / 148 / 148 and triangles 252,269 / 464,477 / 412,200 / 784,059. SwiftShader first-frame times were 4,919 / 8,348 / 3,197 / 4,574 ms; the Chapter XII Balanced sample also recorded a 381 ms combat long task. These software timings are diagnostics, not hardware performance results; the shader gate does not certify a frame-rate target.

The equipped-model preview was inspected and rotated in the Windows browser. Headless Chromium captured its initial rendered model, but subsequent screenshot capture stalled despite a visible, focused page, continuing animation frames, responsive controls and unchanged canvas dimensions. Disabling CSS animations did not resolve it, and the alternate compositor capture failed explicitly. The automated kit check therefore verifies model loading, rotation/reset input, stable layout, closing and unchanged battle time/pause; it does **not** claim an automated pixel comparison of the rotated model. That visual behavior is covered by the Windows manual check.

### Windows GPU measurement

The built-in camera-tour check ran in the real-time Chapter V mine encounter on the final build, in the Windows in-app browser at a 1,265 × 712 viewport. It reported Intel Graphics through ANGLE / Direct3D 11, not a software renderer. Automated browser rendering was stopped during this measurement. Each quality rebuilt the current scene and toured overview, follow and horizon cameras; the simulation stayed paused. The built-in check disables adaptive resolution during measurement and restores the player's setting afterward.

| Quality | Scene build | Typical rate | Frame p95 | GPU p95 | Longest freeze | Built-in target result |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Balanced | 3,074 ms | 59.9 fps | 33 ms | 19 ms | 0 ms | Below 60 fps |
| High | 3,924 ms | 29.9 fps | 67 ms | 37 ms | 71 ms | Below 60 fps |
| Performance | 2,527 ms | 30 fps | 34 ms | 23 ms | 0 ms | Meets 30 fps |

The game recommended Performance, which remains selected in the preview. Balanced has a near-60 average but does not sustain the frame-time target; High still needs further GPU optimization on this device. These results cannot be compared as a percentage improvement with the original playtest, which measured Chapter II in a much narrower viewport. This is one crowded scene and camera tour, not twelve-chapter hardware certification or a measurement of an uninterrupted running battle.

The warning-only art handoff audit reports that exported handoff GLBs are stale after the character source changes. Run `npm run art:handoff` before the next external art handoff. This does not affect the audited runtime assets or the game build.

Local detailed logs are in `tests/visual-results/release-verification-20261022/`, `tests/visual-results/realtime-polish/` and `tests/visual-results/realtime-polish-chapters/`. These ignored test outputs are not bundled into the game. The production build remains within its download budgets: 763,095 initial gzip bytes, 9,847,464 Chapter I gzip bytes and 18,292,185 complete-campaign gzip bytes.

## Remaining qualification

Physical phone checks and first-time player sessions still need the owner's arranged testers. An uninterrupted manual real-time campaign, audio assessment and controller support have not been qualified by this work. Automated legal victories establish campaign completion and regressions, not enjoyment, difficulty balance or the 90–150 minute human play target. Software-rendered chapter and shader checks do not establish GPU frame rates on a real device.
