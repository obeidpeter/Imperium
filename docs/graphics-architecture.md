# Graphics, loading, and offline architecture

The presentation uses local WebP atlases, Canvas 2D, and DOM/CSS narrative views. No GPU framework, external font, asset CDN, analytics, or runtime package was added. Rules and saved campaign state remain separate from rendering; visual paths, poses, particles, and lighting do not award or change game outcomes.

## Rendering and motion

`src/ui/renderer.js` retains the legal board geometry. Figure-body picking adds a larger visible target above a unit's actual tile; ground picking still resolves through the same isometric coordinates. `battle-art.js` composes cached continuous ground, depth-sorted scenery and units, contact shadows, side/selection markers, bars, and action effects. Terrain, target lists, keyboard controls, and legal actions remain driven by encounter state. The v0.8 presentation adds local material, prop and authored animation sheets alongside the retained artwork and deterministic fallbacks.

`unit-sprites.js` selects artwork using actual military faction, role, chapter, escort identity and facing. It does not mirror a facing, which would reverse weapon and shield hands. `sprite-frames.js` selects an authored sequence only when costume, action and direction match. Five animation sheets contain 156 generated frames: recruit walk/strike/brace/recoil/throw, Roman infantry walk/strike, Gothic infantry walk/strike, and senatorial speech. Coverage is intentionally partial; cavalry, officers, eastern equipment, and uncovered directions retain the existing four-facing art and procedural poses. Missing art returns control to the Canvas figure fallback.

`sprite-poses.js` samples explicit procedural keyposes for strike, throw, brace, recoil, speech, and their command/mounted variants. Each action has preparation, emphasis, and recovery and returns to rest. Walking and running alternate a lifted foot with a planted foot; a distance-based `stridePhase` supplied by the timeline synchronizes the cycle with the reconstructed visual path. Robed figures use a smaller gait and riders use a different weight shift. The motion is deliberately bounded because the source images are complete painted silhouettes, not separated skeletal parts.

The authored catalog records measured connected-alpha source rectangles, convex clips and sole anchors because the generated sheets do not have perfectly uniform spacing. Frame selection reuses unchanged source pixels; it does not repair pixels or rearrange the exported sheet. For uncovered actions, active articulation uses a continuous 30-vertex, 40-triangle mesh over the original facing cell. Shared vertices preserve a connected silhouette and bounded displacement limits folding and reach. This fallback moves existing equipment pixels together without drawing a duplicate arm or weapon. Settled fallback poses use a single image stamp, and turns may blend the two original facing images. The mixed library should not be described as a complete authored animation set for every unit.

`ground-materials.js` samples six material cells with screen-continuous coordinates, so adjacent matching ground does not restart its texture at each tile. `world-art.js` extends actual edge materials beyond the board and uses one broad light field with a dark perimeter. `terrain-detail.js` adds shore and marsh edges, paved verges, tracks and sparse regional details. Three world sheets provide materials, natural props, timber defenses and supplies. Raised terrain remains depth-sorted and fades when it covers the selected figure. Political floors use a continuous chamber composition. The idle lattice is hidden; targets, hover, keyboard focus and the explicit Grid option retain tactical outlines. All decoration is deterministic and neither adds blockers nor changes visibility, passability, objectives or gameplay randomness. Performance mode omits optional patches and extra set dressing. See [world provenance](production-world-art.md).

`battle-effects.js` draws short projectile flights, weapon arcs, contact flashes, shield sparks, surface-aware movement trails, and quieter speech cues from the committed timeline. Projectile angle follows its visual flight tangent. Trails follow reconstructed path segments rather than a straight shortcut across blocked terrain. Effect counts are bounded by the existing quality budgets; Performance omits nonessential trails and weapon arcs. These cues illustrate recorded actions and changes rather than simulating another combat result.

`motion.js` and `animation-timeline.js` animate detached before/after encounter snapshots. The UI captures the submitted command before it clears selection. A rerender, modal, hidden document, resize, or another command cancels the active player and restores the final static board. Reduced motion disables the action animation.

The command stage opts into continuous visible atmosphere through `ambient.js`: callbacks are capped at 12 frames/second in Balanced and 20 in High. Performance and reduced-motion settings disable it. Hiding, detachment, scroll, resize, modal opening or another command cancels it; the UI owns any restart after interruption. Action animation takes priority. The module's default for callers without `continuous:true` remains a six-second arrival beat. CSS scene animation pauses while the document is hidden. These callback caps are not measured display frame rates or a battery-life guarantee.

## Graphics settings

`graphics-quality.js` exports `GRAPHICS_MODES`, `defaultGraphicsMode`, and `createGraphicsQuality`. Settings store the effective mode locally.

| Mode | Canvas pixel-ratio cap | Optional particle budget | Shadow layers | Atmosphere |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Performance | 1 | 0 | 1 | off |
| Balanced | 1.5 | 6 | 2 | on |
| High | 2 | 12 | 3 | on |

The default is Balanced. Save-Data or a valid device-memory hint of 2 GB or less starts in Performance. These hints are conservative choices, not measurements of the device. An explicit saved/player choice takes precedence. The controller retains at most 30 draw-cost samples. When at least 18 of 30 active samples exceed 22 ms, it lowers one step and clears that sample window. Hidden, inactive, and invalid samples do not count. It never raises quality automatically; the player can select another mode. These are synchronous drawing costs, not a claim about frame rate or physical-device performance.

## Artwork API and bounded retention

`art.js` exports `loadArt`, `peekArt`, `artCacheStats`, `clearArtCache`, `chapterAssets`, and `prefetchChapter`, plus the existing portrait/scene helpers. `speakerPortrait(speaker, 'intense')` selects an expression variant without changing character identity.

`art.js` configures the loader from `presentation-budget.js`: **40 entries and 64 MiB estimated RGBA bytes**, whichever limit is reached first. `asset-loader.js` deduplicates pending loads and decodes at most two images concurrently. Failed loads return `null`, use a 15-second retry delay, and can be explicitly retried. Decoding has a 15-second timeout. Oversized images can be returned to a caller but are not retained in this cache. Eviction drops this cache's reference; it does not invalidate an image already being drawn.

Those caps describe application-retained image references and width × height × 4 estimates. DOM image elements, in-flight decodes, Canvas backing stores, browser HTTP caches, and browser/GPU allocations are separate. This is not a total-process memory cap.

`chapterAssets(chapter)` lists scene/current and promotion portraits, ledger portraits, reachable expressions, the oath seal, and that chapter's tactical, world and animation atlases. `prefetchChapter(chapter, {onProgress, retry, next, connection, shouldPrefetchNext})` decodes the current group. Its progress reports completed, total, failed, and percent. Optional next-chapter warming fetches compressed responses only, at most two at once, so it cannot fill the decoded-image cache with future artwork. It is disabled for Save-Data, 2G/slow-2G, or downlink below 1 Mbps, and stops queuing when the request is superseded or the caller says it is no longer relevant. Missing art leaves the readable tactical fallback in place; Settings offers an explicit current-chapter retry.

## Offline protocol and build gates

The service worker initially caches the application shell plus the title, recruit, and Varro images. The oath seal and later portraits are excluded from that opening set. The UI requests the current chapter on entry; it does not automatically request the complete campaign. Settings' **Download all twelve chapters** control explicitly requests the full download.

| Request to the active worker | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `{type: 'cache-chapter', chapter}` | Cache shell plus that chapter's declared assets. |
| `{type: 'offline-status', chapter}` | Report complete-campaign and current-chapter readiness. |
| `'cache-campaign'` | Download every declared production asset after the player's request. |

`chapter-offline` responses contain `{chapter, ready, completed, total, percent, revision}`. Full progress uses `offline-progress`; `offline-ready` is sent only when all production files are present. Errors identify the failed chapter or complete request and allow retry. The header distinguishes current-chapter readiness from all-chapter readiness. Optional prefetch completion alone is never treated as proof that a chapter is available offline.

Each build uses a content revision and an edition-scoped cache. Reads and readiness checks use that cache alone. Installation fetches with HTTP cache revalidation bypass (`cache: 'reload'`) so an old image at the same URL cannot satisfy the new edition. A waiting worker does not call `skipWaiting`; old tabs finish before activation and old-cache cleanup. Production navigation can restore the cached shell. A source preview does not include generated `sw.js` and therefore does not prove PWA behavior.

`scripts/build.mjs` produces chapter groups and `dist/build-report.json`, fails on missing required artwork, and enforces the v0.8 limits in `presentation-budget.js`: **2,000,000 compressed bytes for the opening install, 8,000,000 for shell plus chapter I, and 24,000,000 for the complete opt-in campaign**. This release raises the chapter/full limits to accommodate additional artwork; the initial cap remains unchanged. Measurements sum individually gzipped production files, including the worker. They are package estimates, not browser transfer traces or ZIP sizes. `scripts/verify-assets.mjs` checks those limits, declared integrity, chapter coverage and worker behavior.

## Version 0.29 presentation systems (3D)

All of these read the encounter and the shared animation timeline; none edits campaign or encounter state, consumes the rules' random generator, or decides legality. Each is covered by tests in `tests/v029-upgrades.test.mjs`.

- **Frame pacing and resolution** (`frame-budget.js`): painting is aligned to whole display refreshes (`createFrameCadence`, 30 fps on Performance, 60 on Balanced/High). Without the GPU timer extension, adaptive resolution counts late frames on the CPU (`recordInterval`) and lowers the pixel ratio in 0.1 steps (minimum 0.65), restoring slowly after a long on-time run.
- **Compositor** (`scene-compositor.js`): a 4× multisampled HDR target on Balanced/High (`multisampleCount`), aerial haze placed beyond the board from the current camera (`setHaze`), a per-chapter grade (`setGrade`: saturation, mid-grey contrast, split tone), and two optional finishes set by the figure style (`setStyle`): a depth-aware tilt-shift (12-tap gather that keeps sharp figures from bleeding into blurred land) and an ink finish (outlines on the near side of depth steps plus soft value banding). Performance renders without the compositor, so these finishes are absent there.
- **Colour script** (`campaign-scene-profile.js`): each chapter profile carries a grade and light balance; `stage.js` starts linear fog beyond the board, scales ambient and sun light, and keeps a rim light behind the subject relative to the camera.
- **Terrain** (`environment-layout.js`, `environment-campaign-terrain.js`): plateau, channel and ford heights are exported constants (`HIGH_GROUND_HEIGHT`, `RIVER_WATER_LEVEL`, `RIVER_BED`, `FORD_BED`). The ground grid is warped denser over the board while its 80 m edge stays fixed for the outer bridge. `createGroundHeight(..., {relief:false})` gives the surface roads follow. Picking ray-marches the relief (`intersectGround`). `batchStaticEnvironment` folds repeated static parts into a matching named instance batch.
- **Backdrop** (`environment-backdrop.js`): a canvas painted in code from periodic ridge profiles and regional landmark shapes, mapped onto an open cylinder (radius 610 m) with fog off. No canvas, no backdrop (tests and workers).
- **Figure styles** (`painted-actor.js`, `FIGURE_STYLES` in `graphics-quality.js`): the painted style draws the existing 2D atlas (`drawUnitSprite`) into a per-unit canvas card. `paintedColumn` picks the authored facing from the figure's yaw relative to the camera, using the 2D board's right/toward convention. The card faces the camera; a colour-masked second card turned to the sun casts the painted silhouette (Three copies the main material's map and alpha test into its depth material, so the shadow card's own material carries them). The overview eases to the diagonal views the paintings were made for.
- **Formations** (`formation.js`): `formationSlots` and `followerCount` are deterministic functions of the unit. `createSkinnedCrowd` draws followers as instances of the leader's overview surfaces through an `InstancedBufferGeometry` that shares the leader's vertex buffers. An instanced offset and turn are applied after skinning (with a matching depth material), and rigid boots, hooves and weapons ride their bone as weight-one skinned parts. Painted formations are `InstancedMesh` cards. `contact-shadows.js` stamps each follower.
- **Readability** (`stage.js`, `battle-3d.css`, `ink-overlay.js`): compact role glyphs for rank-and-file units (`labelRole`, `namedFigure`); an ink canvas draped over the board paints the action's legal cells and, for an inspected or hovered enemy, `threatEnvelope` cells; Senate trims use `BLOC_TRIM` and idle delegates take `assent`/`dissent` poses (`voterPosture`, `actor-pose.js`).
- **Signature moments**: `environment-finale.js` (Aurelian curtain and massed legions for the `rome-approach` staging), `discessioPlan`/`divisionStep` (the division after a decided vote), `actionFraming`/`sideOnYaw` (the action camera; off with reduced motion, focus mode or the setting), and `ceremony.js` with `ceremony-controller.js` (oath and accession tableaux on synthetic boards, mounted over their paintings, which remain as the fallback). `campaign-map.js` draws the painted Tabula in SVG; reduced motion leaves its current leg drawn.
- **Harnesses**: `tests/campaign-3d-browser.html` can select the figure style and preview the hero's move reach or the first enemy's threat; `tests/ceremony-browser.html` shows both ceremonies. `scripts/visual-regression.mjs` records and compares per-machine screenshot baselines of both harnesses.

## Verification scope

Direct module tests cover cache deduplication/concurrency/LRU bounds, failure and retry, compressed-only next-chapter prefetch, conservative quality defaults, bounded cost samples and downgrade behavior, worker chapter/full readiness, stale-edition exclusion, interrupted download recovery, animation cancellation, and UI settings. Pose tests cover recovery to rest, alternating foot contact, restrained robe/rider variants, finite optional inputs, non-inverted mesh triangles, bounded displacement, atlas identity, immutable inputs, and the single-stamp settled path. Terrain and effect tests check deterministic planning, bounds, path fidelity, geometry, and Canvas-state restoration. These are source/geometry checks, not evidence of pleasing movement or freedom from pixel-level seams.

`tests/asset-browser.html` exercises the actual local image library and reports retained-cache statistics. `tests/graphics-lab.html` and `tests/visual-benchmark.html` provide development-only browser inspection and measurement surfaces. `tests/graphics-sprites.html` isolates sampled motion across four facings for visual seam and pose review; these are labelled pose fixtures, not a claimed legal campaign run. The development surfaces are excluded from the production copy.

This architecture note makes no benchmark, battery-life, browser-process-memory, real-device, or frame-rate claim. Final release measurements and observed browser checks belong in the verification report. Real iOS/Android installation, VoiceOver, low-memory eviction, and sustained physical-device play require the explicit playtest matrix in `playtest-kit.md`.
