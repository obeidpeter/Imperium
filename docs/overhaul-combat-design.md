# Combat overhaul design — version 2

Written before mechanics edits for the new graphics/gameplay brief. This is a bounded redesign of the existing Standard difficulty, not a new campaign. The implementation remains browser-independent JavaScript ES modules; the brief’s description of an existing TypeScript core does not match this repository.

## Observed starting weaknesses

The v0.6 enemy loop selects mostly by distance. Guards have saved anchors but do not use them to defend a location. Archers seek elevation only when an opponent is already adjacent. Enemies damage stores only if pursuing a soldier happens to bring them beside the objective. Allies commonly chase the nearest opponent during escort missions. These behaviors are observable in `enemyTurn`, `actAlly` and their narrow position sorts in `src/core/battle.js`.

Player and allied attacks lower health but not enemy morale; enemy attacks lower friendly morale. Broken allies wait indefinitely without an order or Rally, while enemies recover automatically. This makes the same visible morale stat behave differently between sides. Wedge is an unconditional damage/exposure exchange and shield wall uses the same all-direction brace as an individual guard. There is no unified, inspectable explanation of the damage calculation.

Existing strengths to retain are the authored mission layouts, escort/rescue branches, actual occupation objectives, deadlines, reinforcement announcements, legal command cap, transactional retry and the backed finale recovery route. Ordinary battles have no artificial minimum-turn wait. Training already ends after the four demonstrated actions. The established 8–15-turn and 90–150-minute figures remain human-playtest targets rather than mandatory delays or measured enjoyment.

## Intended changes and explicit deviations

- New battles carry `combatVersion: 2`. Mission saves without this field retain v0.6 rules and text through explicit version branches. Their retry options preserve `combatVersion: 1`; old pre-mission encounters still use the existing legacy module. No silent conversion of an unfinished battle occurs.
- One deterministic scored planner examines legal reachable positions, visible opponents, health, role, terrain, facing, nearby support and the current objective. Stable iteration breaks ties; planning consumes no RNG. Enemy guards defend anchors/approaches, archers maintain useful range and cover, cavalry prefer exposed flanks and open movement, raiders value convoy/store pressure, and Roman cohorts prefer supported approaches. Allies screen escorts and contest marked ground instead of always chasing. Intent strings describe the best current plan, not a promise that an enemy ignores later player actions.
- The first road assignment uses the planner’s direct, announced approach policy for opponents and a companion who stays within two tiles of the recruit when possible. A simple beginner diagnostic initially lost all three seeds against full archer repositioning and unsupported allied charges; the learning encounter should teach guarding and target choice before requiring pursuit of a kiting archer. Later archers retain full positional evaluation. This is a decision-quality adjustment, not a hidden health/damage handicap or a separate difficulty mode.
- Structured balance data owns the new weights and combat modifiers. No enemy health or base-damage inflation is planned. Archer height advantage, ranged cover, flank-sensitive formation support and cavalry movement on clear terrain give positioning useful consequences. Walls remain traversable defended positions, rivers/rock remain blocked, and existing marsh movement costs remain intact.
- Individual Brace remains a two-point damage reduction for predictable early play. Shield wall limits marching to one tile and gains an additional frontal benefit only beside another living shield-wall member. Line preserves marsh mobility, reduces morale loss and gains ranged screening when cohesive. Wedge retains its one-point exposure, but its attack benefit requires firm open terrain. Mounted charges require actual movement and do not operate from marsh, ford, walls or broken ground.
- Engagement refinement after the first implementation test: entering a visible hostile melee unit’s adjacent tile ends movement. A unit may leave an engaged starting tile, so withdrawal remains possible. This creates a real screening line; it does not add reaction attacks or hidden movement traps. Ranged specialists and neutral columns do not project engagement. Preview paths must use the same helper.
- Objective pressure is applied once per round when enemies occupy the objective approach without a friendly screen. Contested ground blocks hold progress but does not multiply automatic store damage by every adjacent enemy. The first stronger-AI route exposed that old incidental pressure stacking could destroy the crossing before a defending line could engage; the revised rule rewards actual occupation and remains clearly announced.
- On the political-settlement finale, rival infantry guard their negotiating perimeter instead of launching a general pursuit. The envoy still must reach the marker, the commander must remain near the meeting and Rally must reassure the parley for the existing two-turn meeting. The separate battle and defection routes retain their aggressive field behavior. This preserves an actual diplomatic objective under the improved targeting AI, especially at low Army support.
- Hits affect morale on both sides. Very low morale penalizes damage and makes autonomous soldiers fall back/regroup; Rally can prevent this lost initiative. No separate fatigue resource is added: supplies, condition and morale already represent readiness without a new meter.
- Cavalry no longer takes automatic health damage merely for ending a turn on eastern sand. Movement, engagement, charge eligibility and threat evaluation provide its terrain tradeoffs. Individual enemy guard/line states survive into the player’s response, then refresh at the next enemy phase; defensive behavior therefore has an observable effect.
- Existing actions and command capacity remain. A controlled-withdrawal order is added as a movement-and-brace alternative, not an extra action. Every commanded participant still receives at most its normal move and action; formation membership is established before participants act so cohesion is evaluated consistently.
- Damage previews expose exact modifiers through a pure breakdown export. Move and command previews mention relevant exposure, terrain or formation limits. Enemy and ally intention strings update when state changes. No hidden opponent position is revealed through public plans.

## API and saved fields

`combatBreakdown(encounter, attacker, target, ranged=false)` returns `{damage, base, modifiers: [{label,value}], aspect}`. `planBattleUnit(encounter, unit, options={})` returns a pure `{x,y,targetId,action,intent,reasons,score}` plan; options can select a legal order/destination or the diagnostic simple policy. The UI continues to use existing action/target/preview APIs. Unit `lastMoveDistance` records this round’s actual displacement for charge previews and is reset with the round. `refreshBattleIntents` writes only derived unit text at committed state changes.

## Verification plan and limits

Tests will cover preview/planner purity, visibility, legal movement, exact damage explanations, formation cohesion and movement limits, symmetric morale, order economy, objective pressure, archer/cavalry/guard distinctions, and version1 compatibility against the frozen v0.6 code. Same-seed simple and tactical bots will report wins/losses, objective progress, surviving strength and turns on labelled controlled scenarios; full campaign autoplay must still use only legal commands from New Game. Existing worst-support finale and branch tests remain required. Bot advantages can identify tactical opportunities, but do not prove human enjoyment, good learning pace or physical-device ergonomics.

## Observed verification results

The final scoped run passed 26 tests: 11 new combat tests and the existing tactical-depth suite. It includes five complete legal finale routes with save round-trips. A shipped checkpoint earned through a legal v0.6 campaign reproduces the exact next-turn encounter/RNG and exact retry layout/RNG under version 1. New battles use version 2.

The diagnostic compares three seeds on eight authored battle maps. Each policy starts from the same state within its edition; terrain hashes match between editions. These are labelled injected scenario starts, with every subsequent encounter command issued through the public legal-command API. The separate full campaigns start at New Game.

| Current policy | Wins | Mean turns | Mean surviving strength | Mean objective fraction | Mean integrity | Allied withdrawals |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Simple objective-aware | 21/24 | 7.375 | 56.667 | 0.875 | 100.625 | 6 |
| Coordinated tactical | 24/24 | 7.708 | 61.292 | 1.000 | 99.375 | 4 |

These results show a win-rate and surviving-strength advantage for coordinated play, with slightly longer encounters and slightly lower mean integrity. Integrity is the raw objective stat, including preparation bonuses; it is not a percentage. Individual scenarios include ties and tradeoffs. The frozen v0.6 tactical policy won 23/24 with the same mean surviving strength, 8.125 mean turns, 91.875 mean integrity and seven withdrawals. Cross-edition differences combine the revised rules with the updated legal policy; they do not isolate one causal change.

Legal campaigns complete all 12 chapters and 25 encounters in 213 turns for seed 275, 212 for seed 17, and 216 for an absent-comrade/absent-mentor branch. The default seed 275 route uses 143 battle turns and has three allied withdrawals, compared with 134 battle turns and five withdrawals in frozen v0.6. This is a modest increase in battle duration, not evidence of measured human session length.

The direct opening policy wins all three tested seeds. A deliberately cautious policy that repeatedly braces while low on health and surrounded wins two of three, down from three of three in v0.6. Its companion withdraws in all three current runs. The opening remains less forgiving of passive guarding; human onboarding and pacing need observation before calling the learning encounter well tuned. The limited seeds, fixed policies and eight-map diagnostic do not establish universal balance or player enjoyment.

Full per-run results, state/map hashes, comparisons and campaign traces are in [the combat evidence](evidence/overhaul-combat.json). Reproduce controlled scenarios with `node scripts/combat-diagnostic.mjs`; `--root=<frozen-source-directory>` selects an archived edition without changing its files.
