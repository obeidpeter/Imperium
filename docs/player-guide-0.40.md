# Getting started

**Imperium** is a free, real-time tactical chronicle set in the Roman
third-century crisis. You begin as a recruit on the Danube frontier
around AD 250, earn military commands, govern a province, enter the
Senate and contest the succession after Aurelian's death in 275. The
campaign has **12 chapters and 25 playable encounters**: battles fought
in real time and political Contests in the Senate's chambers, joined by
camps you walk through, people you talk to and errands you can take up.

You always play one soldier in your own hands: you click where he goes,
whom he talks to and whom he fights. Companions march with you from
Chapter III, and from Chapter IV squads and later cohorts follow the
orders you give. The world runs in real time, but it stops whenever you
press **P**, and every command in the game can be given while it is
stopped. The rule of the game is simple: **play your own soldier; pause
to think.**

## Finding what you need

**New game** offers **Real-time with pause** and **Turn-based**. Real time
is recommended for a first game; pause whenever you want to plan. Turn-based
play advances when you end your turn. A previous mode choice stays selected.
Loading an existing chronicle never changes its controls automatically.
In camp, **Settings → Controls → Campaign controls** changes the next
encounter's mode. During an encounter the chooser is locked, preserving
its controls and exact Retry checkpoint.

The **Menu** pauses play. Choose **Resume game** to return, **Soldier &
company** for equipment and training, **Campaign** for your mission and
records, **Save & load**, **Settings**, or **How to play**. Historical
notes, credits and the title screen are under **More**. Every panel has
a **Menu** return button and a **Close** button; **Esc** closes it too.

Settings has five categories: **Readability**, **Controls**, **Graphics**,
**Sound**, and **Saves & offline**. Changes save automatically. Keyboard
shortcuts, mouse preferences and automatic pause have their own expandable
sections. Developer controls are separate under **Controls → Developer tools**.

During play, your objective, progress, deadline and urgent warnings stay
visible. Expand **Mission details** for the full orders. The main action
bar shows common actions; **More actions** reveals the others. Their
number keys still work and reveal the selected action. **Options → Time
controls** contains half speed and one-round stepping. **All actions**
offers every action as a text list. Tips, camera controls and the battle
log expand only when you want them.

In a walkable camp, the next mission and any departure requirement appear
once above the world. **Camp services** jumps to preparations and people;
**Camp briefing** below the world holds the local description. The camera
selector and **Pause/Resume** remain beside the scene.

Optional exercises appear when deployment, squad orders, engineering or
political commitments become available. Open the **Tip** to follow a short
task. Its steps advance only after the actual placement, delivered order,
completed work or accepted speech. Political practice accepts an argument
without a promise; it never requires a debt. **Skip this exercise** has no
campaign cost. **Continue learning** acknowledges a completed exercise.
**Settings → Readability → Replay optional exercises** restores them;
progress is a device preference, separate from the chronicle and Retry.
Once they are done or skipped, each later battle with a company opens
with a short tip on its tactic until your first order.

**How to play** starts with three basic actions and a search box. Search
for a task such as attack, equipment or saving, or open a guide topic.
The full guide remains available offline.
## The title screen

- **Play now** starts a new chronicle. Give your recruit
  a Roman name (up to 40 characters) or keep Aurelius Marcellus, choose
  his cloak colour and his **training**, and choose a difficulty. You
  begin as a *tiro*, a recruit with no command authority: only your own
  soldier is yours to control. **Restore a save code** loads a chronicle
  from a save code instead.

- When this device holds a chronicle, the title screen shows it: your
  soldier, his chapter, rank and place, and how many of the twelve
  chapters are complete. **Continue your chronicle** restores the latest
  valid save. **Begin a new chronicle** starts again, and offers to
  export the current one first.

- **Try a standalone skirmish** opens one separate 3D battle with the
  full real-time controls. It never changes your chronicle or grants
  campaign progress.

## Your recruit

**Appearance** sets the colour of his cloak. In 3D your soldier wears a
generated body that matches his armour, with its own face, hair and
tunic: a recruit's tunic until he puts on mail or scale, then a
legionary's, an officer's from Chapter IV and a commander's from Chapter
VII. The preview shows him in a cloak of the chosen colour; the
quartermaster sells one from Chapter I.
**Training** sets your starting attributes (see **Your soldier**) and
the optional errand you are offered in Chapter II or III. It never
locks kit or techniques: any soldier can learn anything later.

| **Training**                        | **Starts stronger in**                                | **Starts weaker in**                   |
|-------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Legionary**                       | Combat +10, Strength +5, Constitution +3, Agility +2  | Perception, Eloquence and Authority −3 |
| **Explorator** (scout)              | Fieldcraft +10, Agility +5, Strength +2               | Eloquence and Authority −3             |
| **Librarius** (clerk of the legion) | Eloquence +5, Authority +5, Agility +2, Perception +2 | Fieldcraft −3                          |

The Legionary is the easiest start. The Librarius fights like any
recruit at first but grows into the command and Senate chapters sooner.

## Difficulty

Difficulty is chosen when a chronicle begins and stays with its
checkpoints. Graphics quality and game speed never affect difficulty.

| **Level**    | **What it changes**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Story**    | Three extra rounds on every battle deadline, reinforcements two rounds later, gentler objective pressure and gentler rival speeches. Enemies take longer to notice you and to react. Game speed starts at 75% and the game pauses itself when enemies appear. The same objectives and the same earned promotions. |
| **Standard** | The established combat and political balance, with company planning and evolving field objectives.                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Veteran**  | Battle deadlines two rounds shorter, reinforcements one round earlier and, from Chapter IV, at full strength, stronger objective pressure and stronger rival speeches. Enemies notice you sooner and coordinate faster. No enemy is hardier or hits harder; evacuation travel time is preserved.                 |

**Iron discipline** can be added to any difficulty when a chronicle
begins: you cannot save inside encounters, which can only be retried
from their checkpoint. It can be switched off later in the **Menu**, but
not back on.

## What you need

- A current web browser. There is no account, no installation and
  nothing to buy.

- A mouse gives the classic controls. Touch screens and keyboard-only
  play are fully supported (see **Controls** and **Accessibility**).

- The 3D scenes need WebGL; without it the 2D isometric view takes over
  with the same rules. The standalone skirmish needs WebGL 2.

- The game saves on your device. Keep a save code (see **Defeat, retry
  and saving**) because browsers can clear their stored data.

- Sound is optional. When starting, **Enable the soundscape** turns on
  music, environmental sound and voiced briefings; each can be adjusted
  later in the **Menu**.

# The campaign

## The campaign loop

1.  Walk the camp. People with something new to say carry a **speech
    mark** over their heads. The mission orders (**Q**) state the
    objective and the new capabilities to demonstrate, and the chronicle
    (**L**) keeps the story so far as illustrated pages.

2.  Make the chapter's consequential decision, usually at the
    commander's table in the principia. Each choice shows its effect on
    your meters and supplies; a promise also shows who receives it, when
    it falls due and what it is expected to cost.

3.  Complete any preparation offered in camp, train, re-equip and choose
    your companions, then march out through the camp gate to the next
    encounter.

4.  Play the battle or Contest. Check the objective and the required
    capabilities: defeating enemies is not every mission's goal.

5.  After a success, choose **Record the result**. This commits the
    rewards and returns you to camp or to the promotion scene.

6.  Complete the chapter's remaining requirements, accept the
    advancement and continue the story.

There is no grinding. Enemies never return once defeated and every
errand is offered once, so the most training anyone can earn is fixed.
The campaign map shows completed chapters and sealed previews of those
to come. Chapter XI requires the completed military campaign, the Dux
appointment and the resolved governorship; Chapter XII requires the
completed Senate challenges and Senator status. These locks always
follow from what you have earned.

| **Chapter**                | **New responsibility**                          | **What it asks of you**                                                                              |
|----------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| I — The Oath               | Your own survival                               | Walk, run, draw your weapon, Strike, Brace and Throw pilum on the drill field; then swear the oath.  |
| II — First Blood           | A wounded survivor                              | Bind his wounds or carry him, and bring the patrol to the rally point.                               |
| III — Under the Eagle      | Specialist recovery work; your first companions | Scout, recover what the legion lost, and set your companions' standing orders.                       |
| IV — Hold the Line         | One commanded squad                             | Give orders, and bring the squads through.                                                           |
| V — The Centurion          | Coordinated squads                              | Put squads into a Formation through the siege's turning point.                                       |
| VI — The Broken Standard   | The legion's morale                             | Rally during the retreat and bring the eagle out.                                                    |
| VII — A Legion to Lead     | A legion and its council                        | Persuade in the council; prepare the camp and Deploy before the battle.                              |
| VIII — Beyond the Frontier | Negotiated passage                              | Choose crossing terms, carry the council, escort the settlers and secure the far bank.               |
| IX — Rome's Sword          | An operational approach                         | Choose a Stratagem before the decisive battle.                                                       |
| X — The Province           | Relief and civil justice                        | Allocate six convoys, give judgment, win the road battle, and use Address and Rebut in the tribunal. |
| XI — The Senate            | Your claim to membership                        | Win both Contests, using Invoke Record and Denounce.                                                 |

In Chapter X the six convoys must include at least one each for the
river, the farms and the town. River relief raises Army Loyalty, farm
relief raises Public Confidence and town relief raises Political
Backing.

## In camp

The world fills the main view. Camp services are below it, with a direct link back to the scene. **Company stories** offers an optional letter in Chapters IV–XII: read the two consequences before choosing, then return after a field action or hearing for the report. Choices are recorded once; their later acknowledgements remain in your career records.

Every chapter has a camp you walk through, each laid out after its own
setting: the legionary base at Novae, a march camp on the causeway, a
fort above a Danube ford, a Euphrates fortress's staging courtyard, a
broken halt on the retreat west, a council camp before Milan, a ferry
assembly on the Danube, a governor's relief courtyard and a portico in
Rome among them. The people of the story stand in the camps of their
chapters, each with something to say about it. Click a person to talk, click a door or a table to use it, and use the map
(**M**) to go at once to any part of the camp you have already visited.
Everything a camp offers is also listed beside the scene, so you never
have to walk to it: the chapter decision, **Battle preparation**,
**People & conversations**, **Travel to a place**, **Courses &
trainers** and **Local report & campaign records**.

Each camp names its places after its setting (in Chapter V the
principia is the command shelter and the canabae the evacuation
shelter), and the map and **Travel to a place** list them in the order
of this table.

| **Place**                                     | **What you do there**                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
|-----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Principia** (headquarters)                  | The commander's table: the chapter's decision, **Consult the War Council**, and the battle preparations (see **Preparing for battle**). From Chapter IV the duty clerk (in Chapter V, the fortress engineer) also opens the preparations. |
| **Quartermaster**                             | Draw and return kit, sell what you took from the field and collect your pay in denarii.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Drill field**                               | Trainers who teach techniques and courses for training points (see **Your soldier**), and the practice post for **Practice completed encounters**. Varro teaches in Chapters I–V, a veteran instructor from Chapter VI.                    |
| **Valetudinarium** (hospital)                 | Heals you fully between encounters and shows when wounded companions return to duty. From Chapter II Capsarius Didius teaches his medical courses here.                                                                                   |
| **Barracks**                                  | The **company roll**: choose up to three companions to march with you and set their standing orders.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Canabae** (the settlement outside the gate) | A trader with the region's goods and news in every chapter, the local people, their errands and the consequences of your orders. Every camp's local report stands here.                                                                   |
| **Camp gate**                                 | March out to the next encounter once its requirements are met.                                                                                                                                                                            |

- **Camps are peaceful.** Weapons stay sheathed: drawing your weapon,
  attacking anyone, forcing an attack and the war cry are refused there,
  and nothing is spent. Of your abilities, only **Bind wounds** (when
  someone is hurt) and **Carry** work in camp.

- **The local report** (in Chapter V, the courtyard report) states what
  the chapter asks of you or, once you have decided, what your decision
  set in motion, and the latest field opportunity or hearing you have
  won in the chapter. In 3D the camp shows them too, as small details
  beside its stores, water and shelters.

- The camp's people go about their work (keeping watch, counting stores,
  carrying water) and stop when you come near. With reduced motion they
  stay still.

- **Consult the War Council** to hear how the officers and counterparts
  who are present weigh the chapter's decision and the coming
  engagement.

- The **company roll** names the small group whose service follows the
  campaign. Safe returns earn commendations; a wounded man spends a few
  encounters in the valetudinarium, and in camp you find him there, not
  in the barracks, until he is fit.

- **The cast in camp** follow the story. Varro stands in the camps until
  his fate is decided in Chapter VI; Lucan or Sabinus, whichever the
  marsh left you; Crispus at the Danube fort, before the council at
  Milan and in Rome; Severianus from Chapter VI, though in the last camp
  only while he backs you (otherwise his messenger or the treasury's
  envoy stands in his place); Aelia Dacia wherever her households are;
  Paullus in Rome, and in the last camp only before the legitimacy
  Contest. What they say follows your decisions.

- The **journal** (**J**) records the decisions that shaped your
  standing, and its **obligations ledger** records what you promised, to
  whom and when it is due.

- **Practice completed encounters** replays a finished battle or
  Contest, with weather, time and difficulty variations, without
  touching your chronicle or awarding progress.

- **Errands** are optional and offered in every chapter: in Chapters
  I–III one to three each (some only for one training path), from
  Chapter IV two each. Once accepted they
  appear in the mission orders (**Q**). An errand may ask you to carry
  something, read a tally or a track, or bring back the right answer: a
  wrong answer costs nothing, and you can look again. Each rewards
  training points, kit or standing, once; five award titles no other
  deed gives. None ever blocks the story.

- **Some replies depend on who your soldier is**: his attributes,
  decorations, titles, training and the army's or the public's
  confidence. They are listed dimmed with what they need until he has
  it, and where an errand needs persuading, there is always another way.

- Named companions' permanent fates follow clearly signalled story
  choices. Losing an ordinary skirmish never silently kills a recurring
  character.

# Controls

## Point, click, act

Imperium uses the classic controls of isometric role-playing games: the
left button does what the pointer shows, the right button uses your
current ability, and **Space** draws your weapon.

| **Pointer over...**                          | **Left-click**                                                      | **Right-click**                                                                                |
|----------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Open ground**                              | Go there. A near click walks; a far click runs.                     | Use your current ability there; abilities meant for yourself, such as Bind wounds, work on you |
| **A person**                                 | Walk over and talk (weapon sheathed)                                | Use your current ability on him                                                                |
| **An enemy**                                 | Attack him and keep fighting until he falls                         | Use your current ability on him                                                                |
| **An object** (gate, brazier, bridge, chest) | Walk over and use it; click an open gate or chest again to close it | Use your current ability on it, where that makes sense                                         |
| **A fallen body**                            | Search a fallen enemy; lift or recover a fallen comrade             | —                                                                                              |
| **An exit** (gateway sign)                   | Travel to the next area                                             | —                                                                                              |

The pointer shows what a click will do: a boot to walk, a speech mark to
talk, crossed swords to attack, a hand to use, a gateway to travel and a
barred circle where there is no way through. The **message line** at the
bottom of the screen says the same thing in words.

Hold **U** to label everything nearby that you can talk to, use or pick
up, or keep the labels on in **Settings → Controls → Mouse & touch**.

## Moving

- Click open ground and your soldier goes there by the best route. A
  click farther than about eight metres runs; a nearer one walks. **W**
  switches to walking everywhere, which is quieter (see **Fieldcraft**).

- Hold the button down to keep moving toward the pointer.

- Before you click, the ground shows the route: a **solid line** to
  where your soldier will stop. If the place cannot be reached, the line
  ends where he would stop and the message line says why.

- The **arrow keys** move your soldier directly.

- **Marsh** slows everyone, **rivers** are crossed at fords and **rain**
  makes slopes slower to climb. The route line already allows for them.

- You cannot run while an enemy within two metres is facing you: you can
  only walk away. Leaving never gives him a free blow.

## Talking and using things

- With your weapon sheathed, click a person to walk over and talk.
  Conversations stop the world while they last. Choose a reply by
  clicking it or pressing its number.

- Replies that depend on your attributes, decorations, earlier choices
  or the evidence you carry say what they need, for example
  **\[Eloquence 15\]** or **\[Corona civica\]**. Replies you cannot use
  yet are dimmed, with the reason.

- Click an object to walk over and use it. Work that takes time, such as
  repairing a bridge, fills a ring over your soldier and stops if he is
  struck.

- Click a fallen enemy to search him. The loot window shows what he
  carried beside your pack (see **Your soldier**).

- **E** talks to or uses the nearest thing; it is the keyboard's left
  click.

## Fighting

1.  Press **Space**, or the crossed-swords button, to draw your weapon.
    Crossed swords appear on your portrait, your companions draw theirs,
    and your weapon's numbers start to count.

2.  Click an enemy. Your soldier closes in and keeps attacking that man
    until he falls or you click elsewhere. A heavy blow staggers him for
    a moment, then he goes back to the same man.

3.  When the target falls, your soldier stands ready. He does not chase
    the next man unless **Keep fighting** is on in **Settings → Controls
    → Mouse & touch**.

4.  Click open ground to step away, or press **Space** again to sheathe.
    Sheathe to talk or to search the fallen.

- Clicking an enemy with your weapon sheathed draws it for you. With
  **Draw to attack** off, the classic rule applies: the click only walks
  you to him.

- **Shift**+click forces an attack on anything: a gate, a barricade, or
  a person who is not hostile. Striking a neutral person makes him
  hostile and is recorded in the journal, and harming civilians costs
  Public Confidence. On a fortified field it also demolishes a repaired
  bridge (see **Field objects**).

- In camp, weapons stay sheathed: the game refuses to draw them, to
  attack or force an attack, or to raise the war cry, and says so in the
  message line (see **In camp**).

- Point at an enemy, or choose him with **T**, to read the **forecast**:
  your chance to hit, the damage to expect after his armour, and every
  modifier that applies, such as a flank, height or rain. Examining a
  target spends nothing.

## Abilities

Every technique, order and speech in the game is an **ability**. One
ability is always **current**: its icon stands beside your portrait, and
the right mouse button uses it.

1.  Choose an ability with **1–0**, or press **S** to open the ability
    bar and click one.

2.  Right-click the target: an enemy, a comrade, a senator or open
    ground. On open ground the ability is used at that spot; abilities
    meant for yourself, such as Bind wounds, work on you when no one is
    there.

3.  While an ability recovers, its icon dims and shows what it needs:
    Stamina, a pilum, a bandage or time. If it cannot reach the target,
    the message line says why and nothing is spent.

- With an ability chosen, pointing at a target shows its forecast:
  range, area, effect and cost. Examining spends nothing.

- The bar keeps two sets of ten places: one for the field and one for
  chambers. To put an ability on a number key, open the bar (**S**),
  point at the ability or move to it with **Tab**, and press the number.
  On touch, tap **Assign**, then the ability, then its place. New
  abilities take the next free number in their set.

- From the keyboard, choose an ability with its number, then press the
  same number again, **Enter**, or right-click to use it on your target (**T**).
  Self abilities such as Brace, Scout and Rally execute with one press, then
  return to Strike. While paused, the action waits for Resume.

- **Quick-cast** (off by default) uses an ability at the pointer as soon
  as you press its number.

## Time: pause, slow and step

- **P**, the Pause key or the **Pause** button stops the world.
  Everything except time still works: point at anything for its
  forecast, open every panel, choose abilities, pick targets and give
  orders.

  Pause also works while a command-list or HUD button has focus. Text
  fields and modal panels keep their own keyboard controls. Rescue
  encounters begin paused so you can identify the casualty and plan the
  approach before pressing **Resume**.

- Commands given while paused are carried out when you resume. Each
  person keeps only the latest command you gave him, and a faint line
  shows what he is about to do.

- **V** slows time to half speed and back. **Game speed** sets the
  normal pace: 50%, 75% or 100%.

- **N** while paused plays one round (ten seconds of battle time) and
  pauses again.

- **Auto-pause** stops the world for you when the events you choose
  happen: an enemy comes into view, your soldier or a companion is badly
  hurt, a squad breaks or is ambushed, an objective changes or an order
  cannot be carried out.

- **Pause each round** stops the world at the start of every round, for
  a game that plays almost like a turn-based one.

## Your party

- Your companions' portraits stand beside yours at the bottom left, each
  with a **health** bar and a **stamina** bar. A portrait with a red
  edge and a warning mark is badly hurt; a grey one with a fallen mark
  is down.

- Click a companion's portrait, or press **\[** and **\]**, to take
  direct control of him. Every control above then moves him instead, and
  your own soldier follows his standing orders. Click your own portrait,
  or press **Home**, to take yourself back.

- Drag an item from your kit onto a companion's portrait, or choose
  **Give** in the item's menu, to hand it to him.

- Companions follow their **standing orders** whenever you are not
  controlling them (see **Companions and command**).

## Squads

Later armies include specialist formations: engineer details in Chapters IV, V, VIII and X, and mounted cavalry in VII, IX and XII. Engineers support nearby work; they do not complete objectives automatically. Cavalry moves faster on firm ground, loses much of that advantage in rough terrain, and starts with orders to screen a flank. Enemy horse move the same way.

From Chapter IV the squads you command appear in the **squad bar** at
the top left, each with its standard, and the same standard stands above
the squad's men on the field.

- Click a standard to select that squad, and **Shift**+click more
  standards to add them. **Shift+1–6** adds a squad to the selection, or
  removes it if it is already selected. On touch, tap standards to add
  them. A click on a friendly standard always selects; it never attacks.

- **Esc**, the **Clear** button or a click on your own portrait clears
  the selection.

- While squads are selected, the right mouse button gives them orders
  instead of using your own ability: right-click ground to advance

# Your soldier

## Attributes

Your soldier has eight attributes. The character panel (**C**) shows
them with the numbers they produce, an explanation and the change from
the next point before you spend training points. Some totals improve
only at thresholds. Every recruit starts with 10 in each of the first six and 5
in Combat and Fieldcraft, before his training.

| **Attribute**    | **What it does**                                                                                                                                         |
|------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Strength**     | Heavier blows; every five points add one to Attack. With Agility, a shorter pause between blows.                                                         |
| **Agility**      | Half its value adds to Attack and to Defence. The main attribute for throwing and shooting; with Strength, a shorter pause between blows.                |
| **Constitution** | Half its value adds to Health and to Stamina. Faster healing and stamina recovery, and shorter staggers when you are hit.                                |
| **Perception**   | How far you see and how soon you spot hidden enemies; accuracy at range; what you notice about people in conversation.                                   |
| **Eloquence**    | The weight of your words: your Resolve, Denounce and Rebut in Contests, conversation replies, and better prices from traders.                            |
| **Authority**    | How far your voice carries: Rally reaches one metre farther for every two points above 10, squads start your orders sooner, and it adds to your Resolve. |
| **Combat**       | Each point adds one to Attack and one to Defence.                                                                                                        |
| **Fieldcraft**   | How close you can come before enemies notice you, and how hard you are to trace when you strike unseen.                                                  |

## What the numbers mean

- **Health:** blows reduce it, and at zero your soldier falls and the
  encounter is lost. It recovers slowly out of combat, and fully in the
  valetudinarium.

- **Stamina:** spent on techniques, and recovers steadily. Heavy kit
  slows its recovery.

- **Attack against Defence:** every blow rolls against the target's
  Defence. About one blow in ten is a **critical**: it ignores the flat
  protection of armour, and when it is struck by the soldier you are
  controlling it does double damage.

- **Pause between blows:** each weapon has its own swing. Strength and
  Agility shorten the pause after it; Restriction lengthens it.

- **Staggers:** a blow that takes more than a fifth of your Health, or
  any stunning blow, interrupts what you were doing. Constitution
  shortens the stagger.

- **Restriction:** the burden of your kit (see **Kit**).

- **Resolve:** your stamina in Contests (see **Political Contests**).

## Training points

- You earn **training points** for objectives and demonstrated
  capabilities, for errands, for commendations in the After-Action
  Chronicle, and one for each enemy your soldier or a companion strikes
  down, up to ten in each encounter. Enemies never return, so the most
  you can earn is fixed.

- Spend them in the character panel: click the arrow beside an attribute
  to raise it by one. A point costs **4** training points while the
  attribute is below 20, **5** from 20, **6** from 30, and so on.

  **courses**: lasting improvements of the kind no attribute gives. A

  denarii, and each course can be taken once.

## Decorations and titles

- Distinguished service earns **decorations** (*dona militaria*), shown
  in the decorations panel (**A**) and on your soldier's harness. They
  always count, and each has a small, stated benefit: a **corona
  civica** for saving a citizen's life adds 2 Eloquence, and a **corona
  muralis** for being first over a wall adds 2 Combat.

- **Titles** reward errands, each with a small stated benefit (a
  **Pathfinder** adds 1 Agility). Some open conversation replies, and in
  the Senate your decorations count as part of your record.

- Companions rise through the ranks as they earn commendations: **Miles
  gregarius**, **Immunis** (1+), **Optio cohortis** (3+) and **Evocatus
  Augusti** (6+), each with small, stated benefits.

## Kit

The kit panel (**I**) shows your equipped soldier, worn layers and pack.
Drag the model or use its arrow buttons to turn it. It wears the
generated body closest to his kit, as he looks in the field: your
shield, weapons and cloak are drawn as worn, and your helmet or cap sits
on the recruit's bare head, but an armoured body brings its own helmet
and armour, whichever you wear. Select a packed
piece to compare protection, Restriction and derived combat totals
against the piece it would replace, then choose **Wear** in camp.
The comparison spends nothing. **Give** and **Leave on the ground**
are available beside the selected item. If 3D is unavailable the list
and all equipment commands still work.

Armour is worn in **layers**, and each layer adds its protection: a
tunic, then padding, then mail or scale; trousers under greaves; an
arming cap under the helmet; a cloak over everything.

| **Place**         | **Typical kit**                                                                                                                |
|-------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Head**          | Arming cap, then helmet                                                                                                        |
| **Body**          | Tunic; padding (*subarmalis*); mail (*lorica hamata*) or scale (*lorica squamata*); cloak (*sagum* or *paenula*)               |
| **Arms**          | Arm guard (*manica*)                                                                                                           |
| **Legs and feet** | Trousers (*bracae*), greaves and boots                                                                                         |
| **Belt**          | The military belt, which also carries your throwing weapons: two pila, or lead-weighted darts later in the campaign            |
| **Hands**         | A weapon (*spatha*, *gladius*, spear or axe) and a shield (*scutum*), or a bow or sling for those with the Agility to use them |
| **Charms** (two)  | Amulets and letters of recommendation                                                                                          |

- Each piece protects against **cut**, **pierce**, **crush**, **fire**
  and **stun** in its own proportions: mail turns cuts well and points
  less well, and padding softens crushing blows. Each protection has a
  flat part, which a blow must get through first, and a share that it
  then removes. Point at a piece for its numbers.

- Each piece also adds **Restriction**. Every ten points of Restriction
  take one off Attack and Defence and slow your blows, your running and
  your stamina; throwing and shooting suffer twice as much. Heavy kit
  earns its place in the line and is a burden on a scouting errand.

- Your weapon's numbers count only while it is drawn. A shield blocks a
  share of the blows that come from the front; blows from the side or
  behind ignore it, and it does not block while you carry a casualty.

- Pieces you cannot use yet are dimmed, with what they need, for example
  **Strength 14**.

- The **pack** is a grid in which each item takes its own shape.
  **Sort** arranges it, and dropping a new piece on a worn one swaps
  them, sending the old piece to the pack, or to the **ground** strip
  below it if the pack is full. Items left on the ground stay where you
  left them in that area.

- The **loot window** shows a fallen enemy's or a chest's contents
  beside your pack; **Take all** moves everything that fits.

- **Coin:** you are paid in **denarii** each chapter, with donatives
  after some victories. Denarii buy kit from the quartermaster and
  traders. They are your own purse, separate from the army's
  **supplies**.

- Documents you carry as evidence for a hearing stay in the pack (see
  **Political Contests**).

## Fieldcraft

- Enemies notice you by sight and sound. An **eye** over an enemy's head
  fills as he notices you; when it is full, he knows where you are.

- Walking is quieter than running, a sheathed weapon draws less
  attention than a drawn one, and cover, darkness, mist and a dark cloak
  all hide you. Fieldcraft makes each of them count for more.

- A blow against an enemy who has not noticed you always hits and is
  always a critical: an **ambush**. If you struck unseen, he and his
  friends may not know where the blow came from.

- **Scout** (from Chapter III) reveals hidden enemies and concealed
  ground around you for a while.

- From Chapter IV every squad, yours and the enemy's, watches the same
  way, keeping its own eye on each enemy man and squad. Your soldier's
  first blow on a man of a squad that has not noticed him, while it is
  fighting no one, is an ambush too, so **Quiet feet** and **Ambush
  craft** count in every battle.

- A squad that reaches another that has not seen it coming strikes a
  whole exchange at once, with twice the usual blow to its morale, and
  the target's brace and shield wall do not help: an **ambush on a
  squad**. It works both ways: in mist, at night and behind cover, look
  for bands you have not yet seen.

# Battles

## The field and the clock

- Battles run in real time. The **battle clock** at the bottom centre of
  the screen counts **rounds** of ten seconds, and deadlines,
  reinforcements and field opportunities are given in rounds. The clock
  stops while the game is paused and slows with it.

- Your soldier is marked with a **star**. If he falls, the battle is
  lost. Companions can fall and be recovered, and squads can break and
  be rallied.

- In the early chapters you control only your soldier and, from Chapter
  III, your companions. Allies act on their own and show their
  intentions.

- From Chapter IV each squad you command stands as a **formation**: its
  leader and his men, drawn up in line, shield wall or wedge. Men leave
  the formation as the squad loses strength, so a thin formation is a
  weakened one. The rules treat a squad as one body with one strength,
  morale and cohesion, and every man you strike down in an enemy squad
  takes one from its strength. Squads in contact exchange blows once a
  second; each exchange varies by up to a quarter either way, so a fight
  averages what its forecast says but no two go alike.

## Terrain and position

- **Marsh** slows movement. **Rivers** are crossed at fords, which slow
  movement too.

- **Walls** and **high ground** protect. A man on higher ground gains
  Defence against those below him, and missiles thrown or shot downhill
  reach farther and hit more often.

- An enemy within two metres who faces you stops you running, so a line
  can screen an approach. Walking away never gives him a free blow.

- **Flanks** bypass a shield front: blows from the side or behind are
  never blocked, and blows from behind gain Attack.

- **Cavalry** need an open approach and a run of at least 20 metres on
  firm ground to charge, and rough terrain weakens the charge. A braced
  front that survives a frontal mounted charge strikes back with one
  free blow, no more than once a round.

- Point at the target before committing: the forecast lists the
  modifiers that actually apply.

## Weather

- Each battle's weather is drawn from its chapter's season when it
  begins: the chapter's own weather most often, sometimes another (rain
  in Chapter III may give way to mist or a clear day). The marshes of
  Chapter II always lie in fog. **Retry checkpoint** brings back the
  same weather.

- In rain, climbing slopes is slower; the route line already counts it.

- Rain, mist and dust take 1 off missile damage and make missiles less
  accurate beyond 20 metres. The forecast lists every modifier that
  applies.

## Field objects

Fortified fields from Chapter IV have their walls, towers and gates where their history puts them: the ditched gateway of Chapter IV, the towers of Chapter V's river fortress, the bridgehead rampart at Chapter VIII's crossing, and the towers and barred gates of Palmyra (Chapter IX) and Rome (Chapter XII).

- **Towers.** Climb the stair to the deck, 3.2 metres up. A man on the deck sees 15 metres farther, and his javelins and arrows reach three tenths farther (from other high ground, a fifth). His missiles hit more often (**From the tower**), and he holds off blows from below (**He holds the tower**). The tower's masonry stops missiles shot past it from below (**Behind the tower**).

- **Gates.** A closed fortified gate stops both armies. You can reopen it, or close it again once the gateway is empty: closing it bars it for your side, at 120 integrity. A gate barred against you cannot be opened. Each work action at it batters it instead, taking 15 integrity, or 30 with a friendly engineer detail within eight metres as the ram. At 0 it is a breach: rubble, slower ground, open to both armies for the rest of the battle. In Chapters IX and XII the enemy holds the gates barred.

- **Weakened walls.** Breaching one takes two uninterrupted four-second work actions and leaves a permanent, slower rubble passage.

- **Ramparts and ditches.** A rampart is a bank 1.2 metres high with a timber breastwork on its face, behind a ditch 0.4 metres deep. The ditch halves speed and breaks a charge; a road crosses it on a causeway. A man on the rampart holds higher ground against those below (**He holds the rampart**), missiles from beyond its face lose 10 (**Behind the rampart**), and a squad behind it takes 2 less in each exchange.

- **Bridges.** **Shift**+click a repaired bridge on a fortified field to demolish it: two work actions, or one with engineers near you, each twice as long as ordinary work. The crossing becomes deep water for both armies.

In Chapter V, the countermine and enemy siege ramp put pressure on the defended line from round four. Securing each takes two work actions. A standing friendly engineer within eight metres, the engineering approach, or engineering support earned in the previous siege completes the work in one action. Stopping the ramp also delays the next reserve by one round when the deadline allows. Protect the main objective while choosing whether to finish both works. The result records unfinished works or withdrawn formations; securing both with the company intact earns support for the next field action. That support only speeds the work: the 10 extra defence for your squads comes with the Engineering approach alone.

Every battle after the training drill offers two opportunities, and you
can take only one. Their effects are shown in the field panel, and both
close at the end of round 7 (or one round before a shorter mission's
deadline). Neither replaces the main objective.

To use an object, click it. Your soldier walks to it and works for a few
seconds while a ring fills, to **Secure objective**, **Open gate**,
**Repair bridge**, **Dismantle**, **Ignite** or **Extinguish**. Being
struck stops the work. Using an object spends no Command and no Stamina.

- A **timber barricade** (from Chapter III) gives defenders within two
  metres 1 extra protection against every blow until it is dismantled.

- A lit **pitch brazier** blocks sight with its smoke and hurts **both
  sides** within three metres at the end of each round. It burns for
  three rounds, or one in rain.

- A **closed gate** blocks movement and sight until opened. A **damaged
  footbridge** becomes a ford once repaired.

- Where the supply cart, the signal station, the barricade and the
  brazier stand varies from battle to battle among the open ground near
  their usual places, as does where reinforcements come on and where a
  hidden man lies in Chapter III. **Retry checkpoint** restores the same
  field.

## Morale, cohesion and suppression

- **Morale** belongs to every group of fighters, ours and theirs:
  squads, war bands and patrols. Your soldier and your companions never
  break.

- Hits lower morale on both sides. A shaken group (morale 40 or less)
  shows an amber ring with a notch at its standard; a broken group (20
  or less) shows a crimson ring split in two, and falls back to regroup.
  Rally, Restore morale and Inspire restore morale.

- In Chapters I–III the raiders who fight alone are one **war band**
  with one morale, led by its **war leader**, the hardiest of them. Each
  man who falls costs the band heart, more when he is struck down from
  ambush and more again when he is the leader; ending a round
  outnumbered two to one costs it too, while a round out of the fight
  restores some and reinforcements lift it. A wavering band shows the
  amber ring at each man's feet and, after Chapter I, its blows take 1
  less; a broken band shows the crimson ring and runs for its own edge
  of the field. A man who escapes is gone for the objective but earns
  you no training points; one cut down as he runs still counts.

- Every squad also has **cohesion** (80 at the start) and
  **suppression** (0 to 3). Blows wear cohesion down; arrows and
  javelins wear it less but add suppression.

- After Chapter I, cohesion below 40 and suppression of 2 or more each
  take 1 off the damage of that squad's blows.

- A squad that holds its ground for a whole round, or has another
  friendly squad within ten metres, restores cohesion and lifts one
  point of suppression at the end of the round. Rally restores more and
  clears suppression.

## Reading the battlefield

- When you choose an ability, the ground it can reach is washed in ink.
  With **Show ranges** enabled (or **U** held while paused), a thin outline
  marks the ground the selected enemy could strike within the next round.
  The target card also describes his wind-up, recovery and a useful counter.

- Pause and hold **U** to see each visible enemy's intention as an
  arrow: where he is going and whom he means to strike. **Allied plans &
  visible threats** says the same in words, and both update as the field
  changes.

- The ring at every man's feet shows his allegiance by colour and by
  shape: a full circle for friends, a dashed circle for neutrals and a
  spiked circle for enemies.

- Arrows at a man's feet show his facing; a guard arc marks bracing; a
  squad's base shape shows a line, a wedge or a shield wall; an
  exclamation mark warns of low morale.

- Rank-and-file soldiers show a small role sign and a strength bar
  instead of a name; point at or target a man to see his name.

- After each encounter the **After-Action Chronicle** names
  distinguished service and turning points and, after a defeat, gives a
  concrete tactical diagnosis.

# Companions and command

## Companions

- From Chapter III up to **three companions** from your company can
  march with you; choose them on the company roll in the barracks.

- Each companion has his own kit, Health and Stamina, and grows on his
  own: his attributes rise with his rank, and veterans with two
  completed deployments can specialise.

- A companion whose Health reaches zero is **down**, not dead. Bind his
  wounds or carry him to safety. A companion still down when the
  encounter ends is wounded, and spends a few encounters in the
  valetudinarium.

- Companions keep their places behind you on the march and never chase
  an enemy more than 25 metres from you unless their orders send them.

- You can take direct control of any companion at any time (see
  **Controls → Your party**).

## Standing orders

Each companion has four **standing orders** in priority order. Whenever
you are not controlling him, he carries out the first order on his list
that he can, and moves to the next when he cannot.

1.  Open the company panel (**R**) and choose a companion.

2.  Drag an **action** into one of the four slots, or select the slot
    and choose the action from its list: Attack, Guard, Follow close,
    Follow far, Stay hidden, Withdraw, or one of his own techniques such
    as Bind wounds or Throw pilum.

3.  Choose its **target** from the list: you, a named companion, any
    companion, any ally, your target, whoever attacks you, the enemy
    nearest you, the strongest enemy, the weakest enemy, enemy archers,
    or an enemy officer or standard-bearer.

4.  Read the summary: the panel says in words what he will do, for
    example *Guard Marcellus against whoever attacks him; otherwise
    strike Marcellus's target; otherwise follow close.*

Bind wounds is used only on someone below half his Health, and Withdraw
only when the companion himself is below a third of his.

|----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Shieldmate** | Guard you · Attack whoever attacks you · Attack your target · Follow close                    |
| **Skirmisher** | Throw pilum at the enemy nearest you · Attack the weakest enemy · Follow far · Withdraw       |
| **Orderly**    | Bind wounds on any companion · Bind wounds on you · Follow close · Attack whoever attacks you |

Veterans with two completed deployments can specialise from Chapter III.

## Squad standing orders

Each squad has four standing orders, set in the company panel (**R**) in
the same way as a companion's. Its actions are Advance with you, Hold
the line, Attack your target, Screen a flank, Guard the standard and
Withdraw when broken; its targets include you, a named squad, the
nearest enemy group, enemy cavalry and enemy archers. The presets **Line
of battle**, **Reserve** and **Flank guard** cover most battles.

## Deployment

From Chapter VII some battles open paused in the **deployment phase**,
where you place one thing at a time. Select a squad's standard (click
it, or **Shift+1–6**), or your soldier or a companion (click his
portrait, or **Home**, **\[** and **\]**). Then click marked ground in
your deployment zone to place him there, or drag the standard; from the
keyboard, move the marker with the arrow keys and press **Enter**.
**Sound the advance** starts the battle clock once you confirm it; until
then **P** and **N** do nothing. Deployment spends no Command. Where
the new missions cross a river, the company deploys on its own bank: the
first two tiles in Chapter VIII, three on Chapter X's road.

# Political Contests

## How a Contest works

- A Contest is a chamber you walk through: the Senate's Curia, a council
  hall or a tribunal. Your two **speakers**, your own soldier and an
  allied speaker, are your party there, and you switch between them as
  you do with companions.

- In Chapters XI and XII the Senate meets in the **Curia**, a marble
  hall around the floor. The senators' benches stand on three low steps
  along each side, and the presiding magistrates' tribunal and the
  gilded Victory at the far end. A wall stands aside while the camera is
  beyond it, so it never hides the floor. On Performance, or if the hall
  cannot be downloaded, a simpler hall stands in its place.

- The session is measured in **hours** by the **clepsydra**, the
  chamber's water clock. In each hour each of your speakers may walk
  anywhere inside the pale circle that shows his reach, and deliver
  **one speech**.

- The hour ends when every speaker still standing has spoken or when you
  **yield the floor** (**Y**). The rival speakers then take their hour,
  walking to members and lobbying them, before the next begins. Nothing
  in a Contest needs speed.

- Your allied speaker acts only while you control him. If you yield
  before he has spoken, his speech for that hour is lost.

- **Resolve** is a speaker's stamina and replaces Health. At zero
  Resolve a speaker withdraws and cannot act, but **his vote still
  counts**, and the majority needed does not shrink. Eloquence and
  Authority add to your speakers' Resolve.

- Each member's **stance** runs from −6 to +6. At **+1** a member votes
  for your motion. At **+3 or −3** his commitment hardens, and reversing
  it takes more effort.

- **Only the vote at the deadline wins.** An early majority does not end
  the Contest, and a tie rejects the motion.

- Use both your speakers: compare staying together with covering distant
  benches, and keep enough votes while demonstrating the chapter's
  required abilities.

## Speaking

- Click a member to talk to him. In a chamber the conversation opens
  where you stand, so talking never spoils your position. It shows his
  bloc, his stance and, once you know it, his interest, with every
  speech you can make to him and the change in stance it will cause.
  **Ask about his interests** is free and takes no time.

- A speech that needs you beside him, such as Persuade, offers **Walk
  beside him** when he is inside your reach circle.

- Choose a speech in the conversation, or choose it on the ability bar
  (**1–0** or **S**) and right-click the member, a rival, your ally or
  the floor. Either way, it spends that speaker's speech for the hour.

| **Speech**              | **Cost**                        | **Effect**                                                                                                                                                       |
|-------------------------|---------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Persuade**            | 1 speech                        | Shift the stance of the member you stand beside. A living allied speaker within four metres adds one point.                                                      |
| **Promise**             | 1 speech                        | Win support with a concrete obligation. One promise per member and at most two per Contest; grain and office promises cost three supplies to honour, others two. |
| **Threaten**            | 1 speech · −3 Public Confidence | Fast support, at the cost of Public Confidence and resistance across the member's bloc.                                                                          |
| **Address the Chamber** | 1 speech · recovers for 3 hours | Right-click the floor: shift the stance of members within six metres of where you stand. Position matters.                                                       |
| **Rebut**               | 1 speech                        | Right-click your ally to restore his Resolve, or a rival to weaken his next speech.                                                                              |
| **Invoke Record**       | 1 speech                        | Appeal to your service record, once per member per Contest. Strongest with service-minded members.                                                               |
| **Denounce**            | 1 speech                        | Right-click a rival speaker to reduce his Resolve. His withdrawal removes his actions, not his vote.                                                             |

## Blocs and interests

Talk to each member, or ask about his interests, before you speak. In
the 3D chamber the members wear plain white togas, and a member's bloc
is shown twice, so it never rests on colour alone: by the trim across
his toga's folded band, from the left shoulder, and by the letter on his
stance marker.

| **Bloc**        | **Toga trim and letter**                    | **Responds to**                            |
|-----------------|---------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| **Veterans**    | Broad crimson band · **V**                  | Invoke Record and Threaten                 |
| **Landholders** | Double wheat-ochre band · **L**             | Promise                                    |
| **Civic**       | Blue band broken into three lengths · **C** | Persuade and Address; they resist Threaten |
| **Chamber**     | Purple with a gilt line · **Ch**            | Talk to each member for his interest       |

Interests matter too: **grain** and **office** respond better to
promises than to military appeals, and **law** responds well to
persuasion and public address. A member leans in with an open hand when
he favours your motion and folds his arms against it. When the deadline
vote is decided, the members divide (*discessio*) and walk behind the
speaker whose side they took; click anywhere to skip the walk.

## Promises and obligations

A concrete promise carries its debt beyond the vote. The forecast names
the commitment and the chapter in which it falls due before you speak.
Breaking a promise costs eight Public Confidence and four Political
Backing; honouring it without enough supplies costs one Public
Confidence for each supply missing.

## Reading the chamber

- The **coalition panel** separates committed members from wavering
  support and shows the promises you have left.

- The **Chamber Whip Forecast** marks supporters **At Risk** from the
  rivals' next lobbying, and the **1-speech flips** within your reach
  this hour.

- Rivals' replies are forecasts if the position holds. Lobbyists court
  swing votes, prosecutors attack weak speakers and brokers contest
  pledged support. Your speeches can change their plans; hold **U** to
  see where each rival means to walk next.

## Policy debates

In each Contest you may table one policy motion from the policy panel
(**Table motion**, one speech). After the rivals' next hour, the rival
offers amended terms: **Accept amendment** or **Defend original
motion**, each one speech; leaving it unanswered keeps the original. A
motion's costs and lasting effects apply only if you win and record the
Contest, and each can be enacted once per campaign.

## Hearings and evidence

In a hearing, the documented cases you hold are **documents in your
pack** (**I**). Which you have depends on your earlier service, choices
and surviving witnesses. Inspect a document (right-click it in the pack,
or choose **Inspect** in its menu) to see every member's exact change in
stance; inspecting costs nothing. To present it, talk to the presiding
magistrate and choose **Present evidence**, or drag the document onto
the tribunal. Presenting spends your own soldier's speech, once per
hearing.

# Preparing for battle

From Chapter III the commander's table in the principia offers
preparations for the next field battle. Each card states its benefit and
its trade-off; costs are paid in supplies at departure, together with
the company's supply package. **Battle preparation**, beside the camp
scene, holds the same choices with their total cost.

## Operational preparations

Successful operations also change the next real-time field. Reconnaissance opens a side gate or reveals one concealed position; captured stores repair a damaged bridge, or extend the opportunity deadline by two rounds; a rested reserve gives protected columns four health and formations ten cohesion. A costly operation with a withdrawn veteran earns no such benefit. These changes are applied at departure, kept in the saved battle, and restored exactly by Retry. Recent field reports explain the results in camp.

| Reconnaissance            | 1 supply   | From Chapter III |

## Knowing the enemy

Enemy plans respond to observed braced lines, weakened or isolated formations, exposed archers and threatened objectives. The intelligence panel explains the selected plan. Strong evidence decides the plan; when the enemy's reasons are mild, it may choose among close alternatives, so the same field can open differently. A plan remains in force long enough to read and counter; hidden troops do not secretly inform this assessment. Protecting an archer already under attack takes priority over a flank march.

The **field intelligence** panel shows the enemy's doctrine, its
**plan**, its current focus and the chapter's opportunities. Visible
enemies work together against one exposed man or squad, the more so if
it stands alone or wavers: archers suppress it, flankers look for a side
approach and the rest hold it in place. A reserve held back early can
still commit when threatened, so always use the current forecast.

- Within its doctrine the enemy chooses a plan: hold you in front, turn
  your left or your right, close on both flanks, feint and draw a squad
  out, or commit its reserve early or hold it back. It keeps a plan for
  at least two rounds; the battle log says when it changes, and the same
  battle brings different plans on different days. Each faction
  favours its own.

- Each band has a leaning of its own (the enemy's focus, the nearest of
  your men, your archers or your soldier) and a **temperament**:
  steady, bold (more heart, slower to notice) or wary (less heart,
  quicker to notice). **Allied plans & visible threats** names it.

- Persian, Palmyrene and the claimant's archers loose one aimed volley
  a round; Persian and Roman bands brace once they have held their
  ground. A wavering band pulls back behind a steady one to recover, and
  a band of foot near its archers turns on the squad that presses them.

- A raider strikes the most dangerous man near him: one attacking him or
  his fellows, a badly wounded fighter, or one pressing his band's
  archer.

- From Chapter IV, on Standard and Veteran, a band of raiders may lie in
  wait near its place, hidden and holding its javelins, unless the battle
  begins with a column to bring out: then the raiders hunt the column.
  It falls on the first of yours to come near, an ambush if you have not
  seen it. **Scout** reveals it, and so does a squad of yours that
  notices it. It does not wait for ever: once one of yours nears the
  objective, or at the end of round 2, it rises and joins the attack.

# Meters, obligations and the finale

Three meters measure your standing: **Army Loyalty**, **Political
Backing** and **Public Confidence**. Army Loyalty grows from the first
chapter, and all three are visible from Chapter VII. The journal records
the decisions that shaped them, and the obligations ledger what you
promised and when it falls due.

## The final chapter

In current campaigns, clean relief, reconnaissance and supply operations provide evidence that the civic, military and landholding representatives value in later chambers. Every two relevant successes add one starting stance, up to two. Honoured personal agreements still matter separately.

In the real-time final battle, two clean reconnaissance operations intercept one rival reserve. Honoured agreements with two different military representatives keep another reserve neutral. Both can apply. The guaranteed costly-backing route remains available without either advantage.

- Inspect the forecast before choosing a route. The army meter affects
  loyal formations and defections, political backing affects rival
  morale and negotiation, and public confidence affects the resistance
  around Rome.

- Every route includes a playable confrontation. A battle requires
  victory in the field; the defection, surrender and negotiated routes
  require holding your army together on the map. Choosing a route never
  awards the crown by itself.

- In the battle (the backer and battle routes) the claimant holds two
  cohorts of his reserve back; they march on from his side at the end of
  rounds 4 and 7 (two rounds later on Story), and the field is not won
  until they are beaten too.

- The confrontation is fought before **Aurelian's walls**, beyond the
  field's east edge: brick stretches still rising behind scaffolds,
  square towers, and the double gate between its round towers. They are
  scenery and change no route. Turn the view toward them and the camera
  comes in to their face rather than passing into them. On Performance, or
  if they cannot be downloaded, a simpler wall stands in their place.

- Higher standing keeps more routes open and improves your position. A
  minimum relief force and a costly backer remain available even after a
  hard campaign, but costly backing creates a serious debt.

- After the accession, read the epilogue and, if you like, create a
  **reign card**: a picture of your ruler's name, choices and route to
  share.

- With the 3D view on, the oath at the start and the accession at the
  end are staged in 3D over their paintings. The camera frames their
  moments in turn: the recruits before the standard, Varro holding out
  the words and your own soldier swearing; the legions before the
  tribunal, the herald, the Senate's witness and the new Augustus. With
  reduced motion it keeps the first view. If you turn or zoom the view
  yourself, it stays where you put it.

# Defeat, retry and saving

## When an encounter goes wrong

- When an encounter is lost, in real time, on the board or in a
  chamber, its result keeps a page telling how history remembers the day
  (**Historical context**). Then choose **Retry checkpoint** or **Load a
  save**.

- **Retry checkpoint** restores the encounter exactly as it began,
  random seed included: the same weather, field and enemy. Decisions
  made in camp before it remain; actions, damage, provisional promises
  and anything else staged inside it are rolled back.

- A fight saved by an earlier version of the game cannot go on under
  newer battle rules: when such a chronicle loads, the battle starts
  again from its checkpoint, as **Retry checkpoint** would.

- A lost political Contest can also offer a patron-funded rerun with a
  new debt; you must still win the vote.

- The final chapter always keeps a costly backer and a minimum force
  available.

## Saving

- The game saves automatically when you enter an area, when an encounter
  begins and when you record a result; look for **Chronicle saved** and
  any storage warning.

- While a save is being written, or a camp is still being set up, the
  camp's choices wait: the decision, preparations, replies and **March
  out** are unavailable for that moment. Menus, panels and walking still
  work. A reply at the gate that marches out waits for its save before
  the encounter begins.

- It also keeps a **resume point** whenever you pause or leave the tab,
  so a phone browser that closes the page cannot lose a fight.
  **Continue your chronicle** returns you to that moment, and the resume
  point is discarded as soon as play resumes.

- **Quick save** (**F2**, or **Menu → Save & load → Quick save**) saves at once,
  anywhere except in a conversation, and **Menu → Save & load** restores it.
  With **Iron discipline** you cannot save inside encounters, by quick
  save or by name.

- **Menu → Save & load** keeps up to five named saves of your chronicle, each
  with a picture of the moment. A save records everything, including
  battle time, recoveries and the random seed, so a loaded fight
  continues exactly as it was.

- One chronicle occupies a device, so **Start a new game** replaces
  it and all its saves (the replaced chronicle also enters the local
  recovery records).

## Keeping or moving your progress

1.  Open **Menu → Settings → Saves & offline → Export save code**.

2.  Copy the code, or download it as a text file, and keep it somewhere
    you control.

3.  On the other device, choose **Menu → Settings → Saves & offline →
    Import save code**, paste the code and validate it.

4.  Check the name and chapter shown before confirming that it replaces
    that device's chronicle.

A code holds your name, decisions, progress and your latest save,
including a fight in progress (with Iron discipline, the fight's
checkpoint instead); the game never uploads it. A damaged or
incompatible code is rejected without touching your chronicle.

## Recovery

**Menu → Settings → Saves & offline → Recover older progress** lists the last three distinct
checkpoints or replaced chronicles; inspect one, then restore it. If
another browser tab saves, use **Continue in this tab** to review the
takeover and load the latest chronicle. Clearing browser data can erase
both the current and the recovery records, so keep an exported code as
well.

## Playing offline

- Each chapter's artwork and 3D files download as you reach the chapter;
  suitable connections also fetch the next chapter ahead of time. **This
  chapter is ready offline** does not mean later chapters are.

- To take the whole campaign offline, choose **Menu → Settings → Saves &
  offline → Download all chapters** and wait for **All chapters available
  offline** before disconnecting.

- If artwork is missing, choose **Retry chapter download**; an
  interrupted full download can be started again from the Menu.

- On iPhone, add the game to the Home Screen from Safari's Share menu.
  Browser storage can still be cleared, so keep a save code.

# Accessibility

- **No action needs quick reactions.** The world stops whenever you
  pause, every command can be given while it is stopped, and auto-pause,
  pause each round, slow time and game speed let you set your own pace.
  Contests advance only by the hour.

- Every action in the field and in the chamber can be given from the
  **All actions** list, without pointing at the scene, and standing orders,
  kit and deployment all work from lists and keys, without dragging.

- The whole game works from the keyboard: the arrow keys move, **T**
  chooses targets, **Enter**, **E** and the number keys act, and where
  your soldier stops, targets, forecasts and events are announced to
  screen readers.

- No action needs hovering, a double click or a held button: hold to
  move and hold to inspect each have a tap or key alternative, forcing
  an attack on touch uses a button, and labels can stay on.

- Sound is never required, and meaning never rests on colour alone:
  allegiance, blocs, morale and states also have shapes, letters or
  symbols.

- Text can be enlarged to 200%, motion can be reduced, the high-contrast
  interface strengthens boundaries and labels, every key can be remapped
  and the mouse buttons can be swapped.

> **Remember** Graphics, camera, game speed and presentation settings
> never change the rules, your targets or the numbers behind a blow.

# What changed in 0.40

Version 0.40 replaces the turn-based battle and chamber controls with
real-time controls in the tradition of the classic isometric
role-playing games, with pause at any moment. The campaign, its
chapters, decisions, meters and obligations are unchanged. Saves from
0.38 open in camp; an encounter that was in progress starts again from
its checkpoint.

| **Before (0.38)**                                              | **Now (0.40)**                                                                                                                    |
